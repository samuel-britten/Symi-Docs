# Deferred forms

Deferred forms preserve an operation symbolically until its variables, bounds, or assumptions are sufficiently known. Use `execute` or the corresponding eager operation when you want evaluation.

## Unevaluated form constructors

### summation

<a id="entry-presentation_swift_deferred_forms_capability_construction_summation_api_analysis_session_summation"></a>
<a id="placement-placement.swift.swift_object.uniffisession_summation.4a767531b0be"></a>
<p class="symi-entry-owner">Explicit context</p>

```swift signature
func summation(
    summand: UniffiExpression,
    index: String,
    lower: UniffiExpression,
    upper: UniffiExpression,
) -> UniffiExpression
```


Build a summation node \(\sum\). `execute` evaluates it when a closed form or
finite expansion exists. For the indefinite anti-difference see
`summation_indefinite` on the calculus page. Definite hypergeometric sums are
closed by **Zeilberger creative telescoping**: a defining recurrence is produced
and verified, then closed to a hypergeometric form when one exists (e.g.
\(\sum_{k=0}^{n} k C(n,k) = n 2^{n-1}\). When no hypergeometric closed form exists
(Apéry-type sums such as the central Delannoy numbers \(\sum_k C(n,k) C(n+k,k)\)),
`execute` returns the verified defining recurrence as a `recurrence_equation`
node rather than declining. Every result is symbolically verified before return,
so a sum outside the closed scope stays an unevaluated `summation` placeholder
rather than a guess.

**Bounds.** The summation range no longer has to be `0..n`. Bounds may be any
integer-affine form in the parameters (\(\sum_{k=0}^{2n}\)) or a `min`/`max` of such
forms (\(\sum_{k=-\min(a,b,c)}^{\min(a,b,c)}\)). They are admitted by *proof*: the
term's own zero extension — a binomial coefficient vanishes outside
\(0 \le \operatorname{bottom} \le \operatorname{top}\) — gives an interval outside which the summand is provably
zero, and the declared bounds are accepted only when they are at least that
wide. A declared range that clips the support is refused rather than answered.

**Parameters.** Any number of parameters is admitted. One carries the
recurrence and the rest ride along symbolically, so
\(\sum_{k=0}^{r} C(m,r-k) C(n,k) = C(m+n,r)\) is closed with `m` and `n` intact.

**Domain.** Results involving binomial coefficients and factorials are proved
on the non-negative-integer domain, under the combinatorial convention that
`binomial(n, k)` is zero outside \(0 \le k \le n\). They are not claimed as
unrestricted Gamma-analytic identities, and the integrality and non-negativity
each result depends on are recorded during normalization rather than assumed
silently.

**Finite trigonometric progressions.** `execute` closes finite linear
combinations of `sin(phase + stride*index)` and
`cos(phase + stride*index)` when the coefficients, phase, and stride are
index-free and the bounds prove a finite nonnegative integer count. The result
is a piecewise centered trigonometric form: the branch
`exp(i*stride) = 1` evaluates the constant progression directly, and its
complement uses the finite Fourier quotient. This branch ledger preserves the
value at removable singularities instead of silently dividing by
`sin(stride/2)`. An unconstrained symbolic range, nonlinear index phase, or
branch budget overflow leaves the summation unevaluated.

**Resource behaviour.** A search that reaches a resource ceiling before its
derived mathematical bound preserves the unevaluated `summation` node. That
outcome is a statement about the search, never a claim that no closed form or
recurrence exists; the same placeholder is also what an out-of-scope summand
produces, so the placeholder should never be read as a negative result.

**C-finite summands.** A summand built from *C-finite* sequences — sequences
satisfying a homogeneous linear recurrence with constant coefficients, of which
`fibonacci_number` is the built-in this release registers — is not
hypergeometric and is closed by a separate route. The admitted class is the
finite state algebra generated by fixed non-negative shifts and affine
subsequences of those sequences, closed under rational scaling, addition,
finite products, and fixed non-negative integer powers, so
\(\sum_{k=1}^{n} F(k)^2 = F(n) F(n+1)\) and \(\sum_{k=0}^{n} F(k) = F(n+2) - 1\) are both
in scope. The anti-difference is *constructed* by solving one exact linear
system in that algebra, not guessed and tested. A separate route recognizes the
binomial transform \(\sum_{k=0}^{n} C(n,k) g(k)\) for an admitted `g`, giving
\(\sum_{k=0}^{n} C(n,k) F(k) = F(2n)\).

**Floor-bounded ranges.** An upper bound of the form
\(\operatorname{floor}((a n + b)/m)\) with exact integers `a`, `b` and positive `m` is admitted.
It is normalized by Euclidean division on the parameter into a complete,
disjoint ledger of `m` residue strata, on each of which the bound is an ordinary
affine expression; empty strata are recorded explicitly rather than inherited
from a combinatorial convention. On top of that, affine binomial diagonals
\(\sum_{k=0}^{\operatorname{floor}((n+b)/(c+1))} \operatorname{binomial}(n+b-c k, k)\) are closed by a
structural Pascal decomposition whose moving boundary terms are each *proved*
zero, giving \(\sum_{k=0}^{\operatorname{floor}(n/2)} \operatorname{binomial}(n-k, k) = \operatorname{fibonacci\_number}(n+1)\).

**Verification.** Every C-finite and floor-bounded result carries two
independent exact checks before it is returned: the construction identity is
replayed (the forward difference in the state algebra, the transformed state
against directly accumulated exact sums, or the Pascal decomposition against its
residue ledger and boundary terms), and the returned expression is then proved
equal to the derived recurrence's unique solution on a complete initial window.
A presentation such as `fibonacci_number(2n)` is admitted only after that
uniqueness argument closes — never by matching a name. When a recurrence is
found but no expression in the language represents its solution, the sum stays
an unevaluated `summation` node.

**Domain.** These identities are proved on the non-negative-integer stratum and
record that assumption; a parameter the assumption context proves negative
declines instead. Zero extension of a binomial is used only where the built-in's
own semantics prove it — both arguments non-negative with the bottom above the
top — and never for a negative top argument, where the built-in is undefined.

### product

<a id="entry-presentation_swift_deferred_forms_capability_construction_product_api_analysis_session_product"></a>
<a id="placement-placement.swift.swift_object.uniffisession_product.2f4593fd98e4"></a>
<p class="symi-entry-owner">Explicit context</p>

```swift signature
func product(
    multiplicand: UniffiExpression,
    index: String,
    lower: UniffiExpression,
    upper: UniffiExpression,
) -> UniffiExpression
```


Definite indexed product \(\prod_{\mathit{index}=\mathit{lower}}^{\mathit{upper}} \mathit{multiplicand}\), a first-class
`indexed_product` binder (rendered `\prod` in LaTeX). The result is the closed
form produced by the product engine. A range the assumption context proves
reversed is the empty product `1`. Otherwise the engine tries, in order:

- **Rational anti-quotients.** Factorial and Pochhammer recognition
  (\(\prod_{k=1}^{n} k = n!\)); telescoping and rational products via Gamma quotients
  (\(\prod_{k=1}^{n} k/(k+1) = 1/(n+1)\)); and exact shift-orbit reduction over the
  rationals and the Gaussian rationals, which discovers an anti-quotient whenever
  numerator and denominator factors are integer shifts of one another
  (\(\prod_{k=2}^{\infty}(k^3-1)/(k^3+1) = 2/3\),
  \(\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}(k(k+1)+1+i)/(k(k+1)+1-i) = -1\)).
- **Balanced rational infinite products.** With `upper = infinity` and an exact
  finite integer lower bound, a shift-free rational residual that splits into
  affine factors with equal factor counts and equal shift sums converges, and its
  value is the Gamma quotient of Euler's finite definition
  (\(\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}(1 - 1/(4k^2)) = 2/\pi\), Wallis \(= \pi/2\)). Unequal counts,
  unequal shift sums, a surviving constant content, a factor that vanishes on the
  range, or a pole on the range are all left unevaluated.
- **Periodic interlacing.** A bounded period generated by an exact root of unity
  (currently \(\pm 1\) and \(\pm i\)) splits the range into complete residue blocks
  which are routed through the rational machinery
  (\(\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}(1 + (-1)^{k+1}/(2k-1)) = \sqrt{2}\)). Every terminal prefix of
  a block must separately be proved to tend to one, so a regrouping that converges
  while the ordered product oscillates is declined.
- **Finite Gamma grids.** A `gamma` multiplicand whose argument is affine in the
  index over an exact concrete integer range is materialised and normalised by
  Gamma recurrence, Euler reflection, and the Gauss multiplication formula
  (\(\prod_{k=1}^{8}\Gamma(k/3) = 640\sqrt{3}\,\pi^3/6561\)). A symbolically complete
  Gauss grid, whose cardinality is proved equal to the grid denominator, is closed
  by the multiplication formula without materialising any factor.
- **Parametric root-of-unity products.** The homogeneous quadratic family
  \(\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(a^2 - 2ab\cos(k\pi/n) + b^2)\) is the complete factorisation of
  \(a^{2n} - b^{2n}\) over the \(2n\)-th roots of unity with \(+1\) and \(-1\)
  removed. The result is the **total** piecewise
  \(\bigl((a^{2n}-b^{2n})/(a^2-b^2)\ \text{if}\ a^2 \neq b^2;\ n\,(a^2)^{n-1}\ \text{if}\ a^2 = b^2\bigr)\),
  because the published quotient is \(0/0\) on the second stratum.

Every result is symbolically grounded in the multiplicative discrete
Newton–Leibniz identity `P(upper+1)/P(lower)` and, for an infinite product, in a
finite partial-product identity whose step quotient and lower endpoint are
verified exactly before its limit is taken.

**Domain.** The root-of-unity family needs `n` to be a **proved positive
integer**: the assumption context must say so, and a free symbol is never
silently treated as an integer. Cancellation across a shifted factor is performed
only after proving that the cancelled factor is nonzero at every integer of the
range, so the same formal cancellation is admitted below a pole and refused on
it. Anything outside this closed scope — an irreducible higher-degree
multiplicand with algebraic roots, a coefficient field beyond \(\mathbb{Q}\) and
\(\mathbb{Q}(i)\), an unbounded or unproved period, a q-product, or an infinite
product whose convergence is not proved — stays an unevaluated `indexed_product`
placeholder rather than a guess.

### product_indefinite

<a id="entry-presentation_swift_deferred_forms_capability_construction_product_indefinite_api_analysis_session_product_indefinite"></a>
<a id="placement-placement.swift.swift_object.uniffisession_productindefinite.705e037b04c5"></a>
<p class="symi-entry-owner">Explicit context</p>

```swift signature
func productIndefinite(
    multiplicand: UniffiExpression,
    index: String,
) -> UniffiExpression
```


Indefinite (anti-quotient) product: the `P(k)` satisfying
`P(k+1)/P(k) = multiplicand(k)`, the multiplicative analogue of
`summation_indefinite`. A rational multiplicand over \(\mathbb{Q}\) or
\(\mathbb{Q}(i)\) is first put through exact shift-orbit reduction, which returns a
purely rational anti-quotient whenever the numerator and denominator factors pair
up across integer shifts (\((k^3-1)/(k^3+1)\) gives \((k^2-k+1)/(k(k-1))\)); what the
reduction leaves behind falls back to the Gamma-quotient anti-quotient.
Index-free-base powers close through the summation anti-difference of their
exponent. Declines to the unevaluated `indexed_product` placeholder when no closed
form is in scope.

### piecewise

*Not exposed by the Swift bindings. Available as [`Context.piecewise`](/symi/python/deferred-forms#piecewise) in Python, [`Context.piecewise`](/symi/wasm/deferred-forms#piecewise) in WASM / JavaScript, [`api::solving::Session::piecewise`](/symi/rust/deferred-forms#piecewise) in Rust.*


Build a piecewise expression from `(value, condition)` pairs with first-match
semantics; `otherwise` appends an always-true final branch. Branches whose
condition is provably false are dropped at construction; if every condition
fails the result is the undefined sentinel.

**Notes:** WASM takes a flat interleaved array
`[value_0, condition_0, value_1, condition_1, ...]`.
