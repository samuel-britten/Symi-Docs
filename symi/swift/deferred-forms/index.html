                                                                          Deferred forms

                                                                          Deferred forms preserve an operation symbolically until its variables, bounds, or assumptions are sufficiently known. Use execute or the corresponding eager operation when you want evaluation.

                                                                          Unevaluated form constructors#

                                                                          integral#

                                                                          Explicit context

                                                                          func integral(
    integrand: UniffiExpression,
    variable: String,
    lower: UniffiExpression?,
    upper: UniffiExpression?,
) -> UniffiExpression

                                                                          Build an unevaluated integral node (indefinite when bounds are omitted). Use integrate/integrate_definite for eager evaluation, or execute on the node.

                                                                          derivative#

                                                                          Constructs a deferred derivative. The variable may be a name or a same-context symbol expression. Use execute to evaluate it.

                                                                          summation#

                                                                          Explicit context

                                                                          func summation(
    summand: UniffiExpression,
    index: String,
    lower: UniffiExpression,
    upper: UniffiExpression,
) -> UniffiExpression

                                                                          Build a summation node ∑\sum∑. execute evaluates it when a closed form or finite expansion exists. For the indefinite anti-difference see summation_indefinite on the calculus page. Definite hypergeometric sums are closed by Zeilberger creative telescoping: a defining recurrence is produced and verified, then closed to a hypergeometric form when one exists (e.g. ∑k=0nkC(n,k)=n2n−1\sum_{k=0}^{n} k C(n,k) = n 2^{n-1}∑k=0n​kC(n,k)=n2n−1. When no hypergeometric closed form exists (Apéry-type sums such as the central Delannoy numbers ∑kC(n,k)C(n+k,k)\sum_k C(n,k) C(n+k,k)∑k​C(n,k)C(n+k,k)), execute returns the verified defining recurrence as a recurrence_equation node rather than declining. Every result is symbolically verified before return, so a sum outside the closed scope stays an unevaluated summation placeholder rather than a guess.

                                                                          Bounds. The summation range no longer has to be 0..n. Bounds may be any integer-affine form in the parameters (∑k=02n\sum_{k=0}^{2n}∑k=02n​) or a min/max of such forms (∑k=−min⁡(a,b,c)min⁡(a,b,c)\sum_{k=-\min(a,b,c)}^{\min(a,b,c)}∑k=−min(a,b,c)min(a,b,c)​). They are admitted by proof: the term's own zero extension — a binomial coefficient vanishes outside 0≤bottom⁡≤top⁡0 \le \operatorname{bottom} \le \operatorname{top}0≤bottom≤top — gives an interval outside which the summand is provably zero, and the declared bounds are accepted only when they are at least that wide. A declared range that clips the support is refused rather than answered.

                                                                          Parameters. Any number of parameters is admitted. One carries the recurrence and the rest ride along symbolically, so ∑k=0rC(m,r−k)C(n,k)=C(m+n,r)\sum_{k=0}^{r} C(m,r-k) C(n,k) = C(m+n,r)∑k=0r​C(m,r−k)C(n,k)=C(m+n,r) is closed with m and n intact.

                                                                          Domain. Results involving binomial coefficients and factorials are proved on the non-negative-integer domain, under the combinatorial convention that binomial(n, k) is zero outside 0≤k≤n0 \le k \le n0≤k≤n. They are not claimed as unrestricted Gamma-analytic identities, and the integrality and non-negativity each result depends on are recorded during normalization rather than assumed silently.

                                                                          Finite trigonometric progressions. execute closes finite linear combinations of sin(phase + stride*index) and cos(phase + stride*index) when the coefficients, phase, and stride are index-free and the bounds prove a finite nonnegative integer count. The result is a piecewise centered trigonometric form: the branch exp(i*stride) = 1 evaluates the constant progression directly, and its complement uses the finite Fourier quotient. This branch ledger preserves the value at removable singularities instead of silently dividing by sin(stride/2). An unconstrained symbolic range, nonlinear index phase, or branch budget overflow leaves the summation unevaluated.

                                                                          Resource behaviour. A search that reaches a resource ceiling before its derived mathematical bound preserves the unevaluated summation node. That outcome is a statement about the search, never a claim that no closed form or recurrence exists; the same placeholder is also what an out-of-scope summand produces, so the placeholder should never be read as a negative result.

                                                                          C-finite summands. A summand built from C-finite sequences — sequences satisfying a homogeneous linear recurrence with constant coefficients, of which fibonacci_number is the built-in this release registers — is not hypergeometric and is closed by a separate route. The admitted class is the finite state algebra generated by fixed non-negative shifts and affine subsequences of those sequences, closed under rational scaling, addition, finite products, and fixed non-negative integer powers, so ∑k=1nF(k)2=F(n)F(n+1)\sum_{k=1}^{n} F(k)^2 = F(n) F(n+1)∑k=1n​F(k)2=F(n)F(n+1) and ∑k=0nF(k)=F(n+2)−1\sum_{k=0}^{n} F(k) = F(n+2) - 1∑k=0n​F(k)=F(n+2)−1 are both in scope. The anti-difference is constructed by solving one exact linear system in that algebra, not guessed and tested. A separate route recognizes the binomial transform ∑k=0nC(n,k)g(k)\sum_{k=0}^{n} C(n,k) g(k)∑k=0n​C(n,k)g(k) for an admitted g, giving ∑k=0nC(n,k)F(k)=F(2n)\sum_{k=0}^{n} C(n,k) F(k) = F(2n)∑k=0n​C(n,k)F(k)=F(2n).

                                                                          Floor-bounded ranges. An upper bound of the form floor⁡((an+b)/m)\operatorname{floor}((a n + b)/m)floor((an+b)/m) with exact integers a, b and positive m is admitted. It is normalized by Euclidean division on the parameter into a complete, disjoint ledger of m residue strata, on each of which the bound is an ordinary affine expression; empty strata are recorded explicitly rather than inherited from a combinatorial convention. On top of that, affine binomial diagonals ∑k=0floor⁡((n+b)/(c+1))binomial⁡(n+b−ck,k)\sum_{k=0}^{\operatorname{floor}((n+b)/(c+1))} \operatorname{binomial}(n+b-c k, k)∑k=0floor((n+b)/(c+1))​binomial(n+b−ck,k) are closed by a structural Pascal decomposition whose moving boundary terms are each proved zero, giving ∑k=0floor⁡(n/2)binomial⁡(n−k,k)=fibonacci_number⁡(n+1)\sum_{k=0}^{\operatorname{floor}(n/2)} \operatorname{binomial}(n-k, k) = \operatorname{fibonacci\_number}(n+1)∑k=0floor(n/2)​binomial(n−k,k)=fibonacci_number(n+1).

                                                                          Verification. Every C-finite and floor-bounded result carries two independent exact checks before it is returned: the construction identity is replayed (the forward difference in the state algebra, the transformed state against directly accumulated exact sums, or the Pascal decomposition against its residue ledger and boundary terms), and the returned expression is then proved equal to the derived recurrence's unique solution on a complete initial window. A presentation such as fibonacci_number(2n) is admitted only after that uniqueness argument closes — never by matching a name. When a recurrence is found but no expression in the language represents its solution, the sum stays an unevaluated summation node.

                                                                          Domain. These identities are proved on the non-negative-integer stratum and record that assumption; a parameter the assumption context proves negative declines instead. Zero extension of a binomial is used only where the built-in's own semantics prove it — both arguments non-negative with the bottom above the top — and never for a negative top argument, where the built-in is undefined.

                                                                          product#

                                                                          Explicit context

                                                                          func product(
    multiplicand: UniffiExpression,
    index: String,
    lower: UniffiExpression,
    upper: UniffiExpression,
) -> UniffiExpression

                                                                          Definite indexed product ∏index=loweruppermultiplicand\prod_{\mathit{index}=\mathit{lower}}^{\mathit{upper}} \mathit{multiplicand}∏index=lowerupper​multiplicand, a first-class indexed_product binder (rendered \prod in LaTeX). The result is the closed form produced by the product engine. A range the assumption context proves reversed is the empty product 1. Otherwise the engine tries, in order:

                                                                          • Rational anti-quotients. Factorial and Pochhammer recognition (∏k=1nk=n!\prod_{k=1}^{n} k = n!∏k=1n​k=n!); telescoping and rational products via Gamma quotients (∏k=1nk/(k+1)=1/(n+1)\prod_{k=1}^{n} k/(k+1) = 1/(n+1)∏k=1n​k/(k+1)=1/(n+1)); and exact shift-orbit reduction over the rationals and the Gaussian rationals, which discovers an anti-quotient whenever numerator and denominator factors are integer shifts of one another (∏k=2∞(k3−1)/(k3+1)=2/3\prod_{k=2}^{\infty}(k^3-1)/(k^3+1) = 2/3∏k=2∞​(k3−1)/(k3+1)=2/3, ∏k=0∞(k(k+1)+1+i)/(k(k+1)+1−i)=−1\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}(k(k+1)+1+i)/(k(k+1)+1-i) = -1∏k=0∞​(k(k+1)+1+i)/(k(k+1)+1−i)=−1).
                                                                          • Balanced rational infinite products. With upper = infinity and an exact finite integer lower bound, a shift-free rational residual that splits into affine factors with equal factor counts and equal shift sums converges, and its value is the Gamma quotient of Euler's finite definition (∏k=1∞(1−1/(4k2))=2/π\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}(1 - 1/(4k^2)) = 2/\pi∏k=1∞​(1−1/(4k2))=2/π, Wallis =π/2= \pi/2=π/2). Unequal counts, unequal shift sums, a surviving constant content, a factor that vanishes on the range, or a pole on the range are all left unevaluated.
                                                                          • Periodic interlacing. A bounded period generated by an exact root of unity (currently ±1\pm 1±1 and ±i\pm i±i) splits the range into complete residue blocks which are routed through the rational machinery (∏k=1∞(1+(−1)k+1/(2k−1))=2\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}(1 + (-1)^{k+1}/(2k-1)) = \sqrt{2}∏k=1∞​(1+(−1)k+1/(2k−1))=2​). Every terminal prefix of a block must separately be proved to tend to one, so a regrouping that converges while the ordered product oscillates is declined.
                                                                          • Finite Gamma grids. A gamma multiplicand whose argument is affine in the index over an exact concrete integer range is materialised and normalised by Gamma recurrence, Euler reflection, and the Gauss multiplication formula (∏k=18Γ(k/3)=6403 π3/6561\prod_{k=1}^{8}\Gamma(k/3) = 640\sqrt{3}\,\pi^3/6561∏k=18​Γ(k/3)=6403​π3/6561). A symbolically complete Gauss grid, whose cardinality is proved equal to the grid denominator, is closed by the multiplication formula without materialising any factor.
                                                                          • Parametric root-of-unity products. The homogeneous quadratic family ∏k=1n−1(a2−2abcos⁡(kπ/n)+b2)\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(a^2 - 2ab\cos(k\pi/n) + b^2)∏k=1n−1​(a2−2abcos(kπ/n)+b2) is the complete factorisation of a2n−b2na^{2n} - b^{2n}a2n−b2n over the 2n2n2n-th roots of unity with +1+1+1 and −1-1−1 removed. The result is the total piecewise ((a2n−b2n)/(a2−b2) if a2≠b2; n (a2)n−1 if a2=b2)\bigl((a^{2n}-b^{2n})/(a^2-b^2)\ \text{if}\ a^2 \neq b^2;\ n\,(a^2)^{n-1}\ \text{if}\ a^2 = b^2\bigr)((a2n−b2n)/(a2−b2) if a2=b2; n(a2)n−1 if a2=b2), because the published quotient is 0/00/00/0 on the second stratum.

                                                                          Every result is symbolically grounded in the multiplicative discrete Newton–Leibniz identity P(upper+1)/P(lower) and, for an infinite product, in a finite partial-product identity whose step quotient and lower endpoint are verified exactly before its limit is taken.

                                                                          Domain. The root-of-unity family needs n to be a proved positive integer: the assumption context must say so, and a free symbol is never silently treated as an integer. Cancellation across a shifted factor is performed only after proving that the cancelled factor is nonzero at every integer of the range, so the same formal cancellation is admitted below a pole and refused on it. Anything outside this closed scope — an irreducible higher-degree multiplicand with algebraic roots, a coefficient field beyond Q\mathbb{Q}Q and Q(i)\mathbb{Q}(i)Q(i), an unbounded or unproved period, a q-product, or an infinite product whose convergence is not proved — stays an unevaluated indexed_product placeholder rather than a guess.

                                                                          product_indefinite#

                                                                          Explicit context

                                                                          func productIndefinite(
    multiplicand: UniffiExpression,
    index: String,
) -> UniffiExpression

                                                                          Indefinite (anti-quotient) product: the P(k) satisfying P(k+1)/P(k) = multiplicand(k), the multiplicative analogue of summation_indefinite. A rational multiplicand over Q\mathbb{Q}Q or Q(i)\mathbb{Q}(i)Q(i) is first put through exact shift-orbit reduction, which returns a purely rational anti-quotient whenever the numerator and denominator factors pair up across integer shifts ((k3−1)/(k3+1)(k^3-1)/(k^3+1)(k3−1)/(k3+1) gives (k2−k+1)/(k(k−1))(k^2-k+1)/(k(k-1))(k2−k+1)/(k(k−1))); what the reduction leaves behind falls back to the Gamma-quotient anti-quotient. Index-free-base powers close through the summation anti-difference of their exponent. Declines to the unevaluated indexed_product placeholder when no closed form is in scope.

                                                                          piecewise#

                                                                          Not exposed by the Swift bindings. Available as Context.piecewise in Python, Context.piecewise in WASM / JavaScript, api::solving::Session::piecewise in Rust.

                                                                          Build a piecewise expression from (value, condition) pairs with first-match semantics; otherwise appends an always-true final branch. Branches whose condition is provably false are dropped at construction; if every condition fails the result is the undefined sentinel.

                                                                          Notes: WASM takes a flat interleaved array [value_0, condition_0, value_1, condition_1, ...].
