Verifies the exact global family of a retarded delay request.

A delay solution is not decided by substituting it into the residual once: the residual reads the unknown on the previous interval, so a single reduction proves the equation on one interval only. What is proved here is the induction itself, in the four statements it rests on.

  1. The equation on the first interval, reduced literally in the original residual, with the retarded occurrences replaced by the declared history and the pointwise jet by the family’s first interval. This is what ties the certificate to the equation that was asked about rather than to the coefficients the solver believed it had recognized.
  2. The history, which is the term of index zero being one, so interval zero of the family is the constant the request declared.
  3. Continuity across each interval boundary, which is the term of index k vanishing at the left endpoint of interval k, so the piece on interval k agrees there with the piece on interval k - 1.
  4. The reindexing identity d/dt term(j, t) = -a * term(j - 1, t - h), an identity in the symbolic summation index. Summing it over j = 0 .. k — the index-zero term is constant and contributes nothing — gives y_k'(t) = -a * y_{k-1}(t - h) for every interval index at once, which with 2 and 3 is the induction over the intervals.

Statements 2 to 4 are identities in symbolic indices, so no interval is checked by example and no horizon bounds the result.
